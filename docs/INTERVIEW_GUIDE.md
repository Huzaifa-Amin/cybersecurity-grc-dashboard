# Interview Guide

## Q: Why did you build this project?

A: I built it to show that I can connect governance, risk, and compliance to real business decisions. This is a realistic problem in organizations that need to prove control maturity, manage risk, and demonstrate evidence to leadership or regulators.

## Q: Why not build a SIEM?

A: A SIEM is a valuable tool, but it is not the strongest fit for my current background and portfolio strategy. This project demonstrates the part of cyber work that is often overlooked but very important: governance, policy, controls, evidence, and risk ownership.

## Q: How does this relate to NIST CSF 2.0?

A: The dashboard maps controls to major frameworks and demonstrates the operational gap between policy and implementation. It shows how organizations identify, protect, detect, respond, and recover in a structured way.

## Q: Why is this relevant to jobs in Europe?

A: Because European organizations increasingly need to show accountability, control evidence, and risk management under regulations such as NIS2, GDPR, and ISO 27001-based programs.

## Q: What is the biggest risk in this project?

A: It is a small-organization operational starter with persistent records, role-based access, control CRUD, audit events, and PostgreSQL deployment. It is still not a mature enterprise GRC platform: it lacks SSO/MFA, multi-tenant isolation, high availability, and load testing. The user-count target is not a capacity guarantee.

## Q: How would you explain this to a non-technical stakeholder?

A: I would explain that it helps organizations understand what controls they have, where the gaps are, who owns the risk, and what evidence exists to support compliance.

## Q: What did you learn from building it?

A: I learned how governance programs combine policy, evidence, ownership, risk classification, and business context. That is exactly the type of thinking employers look for in GRC and compliance-focused roles.
